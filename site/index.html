Our Approach

What is integrative dentistry?

At Maine Integrative Dentistry, we see the big picture of how your oral health fits in with your overall health. We want to help you have a great smile, eat comfortably, and be healthy — keeping your natural teeth for your whole life. We use up-to-date technologies and research-based methods, and when your care calls for expertise beyond our scope, we maintain a trusted referral network of specialists.

Our philosophy

Your teeth work hard every day — chewing forces, jaw movement, and the acidity of what we eat and drink all place stress on them. With so many of us now living into our 80s and 90s, we know our patients will be with us for many decades, and we take the long view when it comes to dental care, focusing on treatment and solutions that will go the distance.

That means preserving natural teeth through quality care and minimally invasive, durable restorations. It means screening for sleep apnea, for gum disease — which research has linked to heart disease and diabetes — and for oral cancer at your regular visits. And it means taking the time to listen, because your care should reflect both your immediate needs and your long-term health.

Comprehensive care

Comprehensive care means we think about your overall health — not just your oral health. Whether your goal is a better smile, more comfortable chewing, or less snoring, we offer a wide range of modern treatments and can often be a one-stop shop for your dental needs. When a referral is the right answer, Dr. Kerr works with a trusted network of specialist colleagues. We work with your insurance, offer financing options, and build treatment plans — in phases when that helps — that are financially feasible for you.

A relationship first

Our first priority is building a relationship with you based on trust, open communication, and a personalized approach to your optimal dental health.

The long view

We plan your care for the decades ahead — conservative where possible, comprehensive where it counts — so you need less dentistry, not more, over your lifetime.

Whole-body thinking

Oral health and overall health run in both directions — healthy gums support a healthy heart, and good oral hygiene can even help diabetic patients manage blood sugar.

State of the Art

Technology that improves your quality of care

Our facility has state-of-the-art technology and we stay on the forefront of the latest clinical techniques. We use low-dose digital x-rays, can create digital 3D images of your jaw (cone-beam imaging), and can make your crown while you're in the office.

We use digital photography to see how your teeth relate to your facial features, so results are aesthetically pleasing as well as functional. Our 3D imaging system lets us carefully plan and design your smile: first we take a 3D x-ray of your mouth and jaw, then we use the computer to superimpose our dentistry into your mouth — before we actually do any procedures.

We also mill crowns in-house. That technology decreases the number of visits it takes to finish your procedure — crowns can often be designed, fabricated, and placed the same day.

Implant Dentistry

Why implant dentistry?

Implant dentistry is a wonderful option for people who are missing one, some, or all of their teeth. Implants look natural, restore your ability to chew, work with a range of budgets, and preserve the jawbone — which naturally resorbs over time after a tooth is lost.

Why we're your best option

Dr. Kerr has practiced implant dentistry for over 30 years. We use the best technology possible, including a digital imaging system of your mouth — a 3D scan that lets us place your new tooth virtually before any surgery. Because Dr. Kerr is university-trained in both implant placement and crown-and-bridge restoration, your whole treatment happens in one practice. Often, we can place your new tooth right through the gum without pain, swelling, or sutures.

How we think about implants

Rather than looking at an implant as an isolated procedure, I think of an implant as an addition to the ecosystem of your mouth.

We plan every case virtually before we begin, handle most cases in-house, and consult with specialists when complex bone reconstruction is needed.

Restorative Dentistry — One-Visit CAD/CAM

When a tooth has been damaged by decay or breakage, it needs to be filled or restored with a crown or onlay. Our goal is always to make your teeth as cosmetically beautiful and aesthetically natural as possible while preserving the maximum amount of your natural tooth structure.

We color-match materials carefully and keep an extensive range of shades on hand; for front teeth, layering techniques create realistic, undetectable results. Onlays — a conservative restoration offered by only about 12% of U.S. dentists — let us treat damage without removing healthy tooth the way a full crown can.

Why we do CAD/CAM dentistry

We've offered CAD/CAM dentistry since 2002 — among the earliest adopters in Maine. A three-dimensional digital image of your tooth lets us sculpt the restoration in software and mill it on-site with precision CEREC equipment. Procedures that used to take six weeks and multiple visits are now completed in a single appointment, with restorations that fit comfortably and look natural.

We treat cosmetic dentistry like an art

While many dentists treat cosmetic dentistry as a simple procedure, we view esthetic dentistry as an art. Whether you come to us for the smallest filling or to completely change your smile, we make sure our work looks natural and beautiful — results built to last.

Clear Aligners

A Clear Aligner is an excellent alternative to traditional metal braces. A series of clear, nearly invisible trays gradually moves your teeth into position — typically faster than conventional braces, and discreet enough that most people won't notice you're wearing them.

Sleep Apnea & TMJ

Sleep apnea often goes undiagnosed

Sleep apnea is an increasingly prevalent disease affecting an estimated 12–18 million adult Americans — and it often goes undiagnosed. It can't be detected in a routine office visit or by a blood test, and it doesn't affect only middle-aged or overweight adults; even children can have sleep apnea.

Common symptoms include waking unrefreshed, daytime sleepiness, memory problems, morning headaches, heartburn, nighttime urination, leg swelling, gasping for breath during sleep, night sweats, chest pain, snoring, and pauses in breathing observed by a bed partner.

So why is a dentist involved?

Obstructive sleep apnea — the most common type — occurs when your muscles relax during sleep, allowing soft tissue to collapse and block the airway. (Central sleep apnea, where the brain fails to signal breathing, is treated differently.) Left untreated, obstructive sleep apnea raises the risk of high blood pressure, stroke, heart disease, diabetes, obesity, and depression. Treatment is typically CPAP or oral appliance therapy — and dentists trained in dental sleep medicine work hand-in-hand with sleep physicians on your care.

Oral appliance therapy

An oral appliance is a custom-made device, similar to a sports guard, that holds your jaw in a position that keeps the airway open while you sleep. Patients like it because it's comfortable, easy to wear, quiet, portable, convenient for travel, and easy to clean. Treating snoring or sleep apnea with oral appliance therapy can help you feel like a new person.

TMD / TMJ

We also evaluate and treat temporomandibular joint dysfunction (TMD), which sometimes goes hand-in-hand with sleep apnea. A comprehensive examination lets us build a treatment plan customized to your joint, your bite, and your symptoms.

Dental Hygiene

More than just a cleaning

Everyone wants a great smile with clean white teeth and healthy gums — and your hygiene visit delivers that, with gentle hand removal of tartar, polishing to eliminate stains, and care that reduces gum inflammation. But every hygiene visit here is also a thorough check of your oral health, because your overall health affects your mouth and your mouth affects your overall health.

What do hygiene visits include?

  • An oral cancer exam — including the surrounding skin of your face
  • A head and neck examination for swollen lymph nodes or thyroid concerns
  • Gum health monitoring, with measurements tracked over time
  • Assessment of the integrity of existing fillings
  • Checking for cracks, worn teeth, and signs of grinding
  • A look at your general health status, so we can optimize the quality of your care

We spend real time with you — understanding your individual needs is part of the appointment, not an afterthought.

We make sure you understand your options

Questions are welcome — during your visit and after it. You can always reach us at (207) 775-0001 or patientcare@drkerr.com. A comfortable relationship with your dentist is part of what keeps your oral health on track.

Anxious about the dentist? We understand.

Dental anxiety is a reality for many people, and we take it seriously. We explain treatment as many times as you need, and we treat you as a partner in your own care. Rather than relying only on anti-anxiety medication, we use systematic desensitization — small steps toward overcoming your fears, at your own pace. For some patients that means starting with a visit to the parking lot, then the lobby, the waiting room, and eventually the dental chair — an approach that can eliminate the need for any supplemental anti-anxiety medication.
